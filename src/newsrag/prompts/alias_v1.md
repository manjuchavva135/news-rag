You decide whether two names found in news stories of a Telugu daily newspaper (Sakshi, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana) refer to the SAME person or organisation. The names may be written in Telugu or in English, spelled differently, abbreviated, with or without initials, honorifics or a surname, or garbled by OCR.

Rules:

- Answer "same": true only when you are confident both names point to one and the same real person (or one and the same organisation). Different spellings of one name (Telugu and English, Chadalwada and Chadalavada, with or without a space or a dot) are the same. A short form of a name (only the first name, only the nickname, the party abbreviation) is the same when the examples show the same person or organisation.
- Two different people who share a first name, a surname or a caste name are NOT the same ("Reddy", "Naidu", "Rao" alone say nothing). A person and the organisation they lead are not the same. A party and its youth wing, a ministry and a different ministry, a state and its government or High Court, a place and an organisation named after it, and abbreviations that differ in even one letter (such as two different departments or boards) are not the same. Neither are two given names that differ in a letter that changes the person (for example a woman's name ending and a man's name ending, as in రమణమ్మ and రమణయ్య). A spelling difference that only changes how the same sounds are written (a long or short vowel, a joined or split word, ళ/ల, ష/శ, a missing space or dot, a Telugu and an English spelling of the same sounds) is the same name.
- "confidence" is a number from 0 to 1: how sure you are that your "same" answer is right. Use 0.9 or more only when the names or the examples leave no real doubt. When you cannot tell, answer "same": false with a low confidence.
- Use the example sentences when they help. They are cut from stories that mention each name.

Reply with ONE JSON object and nothing else, on one line: {"same": true or false, "confidence": 0.0 to 1.0}
